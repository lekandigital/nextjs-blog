Spicy food is a global phenomenon, but what actually causes the sensation of heat? The answer lies in a chemical compound called **capsaicin**.

## The Science of Heat

Capsaicin binds to receptors in your mouth that are responsible for detecting heat (temperature). This tricks your brain into thinking your mouth is physically burning, triggering a cooling response like sweating.

## Scoville Scale

Peppers are measured on the Scoville Heat Scale:

| Pepper | Scoville Units (SHU) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bell Pepper | 0 |
| Jalapeño | 2,500 - 8,000 |
| Habanero | 100,000 - 350,000 |
| Carolina Reaper | 1,500,000+ |

## Why We Love It

Despite the pain, many people enjoy spicy food. It releases endorphins, the body's natural painkillers, creating a sense of euphoria.

So next time you eat a spicy taco, remember: it's all just chemistry!
